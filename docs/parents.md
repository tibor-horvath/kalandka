# For parents

## Parents' area

The parents' area opens through a gate (a two-digit multiplication such as 6 × 9 on a keypad, which 5–7 year olds cannot do yet). It is a separate full-screen space with a menu: **Áttekintés** (today's time ring, the last 7 days, plain-language notes on what is going well or hard, progress per game; every game keeps its last few solved tasks and whether each needed help, so each game gets a note: going well, could use help, or too little play this week, with an idea to try at home), **Gyerkőcök** (up to six children on one device, each with their own figure, age, garden, levels and play time; whether Csepi asks "Ki játszik?" at start; removing a child), **Képernyőidő** (daily limit, +10 minutes, rest now, warning and finish-the-task switches, evening rest), **Szintek** (a starting level per game with −/+ steppers, all games open), **Jutalmak** (below), **Nyomtatás** (below) and **Beállítások** (Csepi's voice and speaking speed, sound effects, privacy, moving every child to a new device by QR codes (Átköltözés új eszközre), a two-step progress reset).

## Rewards explained

**Jutalmak** explains seeds, bolts, bells and stickers to grown-ups; the child never sees it. Three rules first (rewards only add up, nothing is random, solved tasks count rather than minutes), then a card each for seeds, bolts and bells: the child's balance, their reward pod as it is now and how many tasks are left to the next one, the rule (every second garden or Óratorony task, every fourth workshop round), what they buy and for how much, all read from the reward code so the page never drifts from the games. A note says finished printed pages count the same way, and that a game with every level done pays at most five a day as practice. The sticker part says a sticker comes with the first task solved on a new level, stays when the level steps down, and names the extra rows (Kiskert, Robotépítő, Kisváros, Feladatlapok); then each world's album rows as a table: stickers earned out of all, and the next one with the level it stands for in grown-up words (the ladder level's name, e.g. "4. szint: Továbbszámolás 20-ig") (src/screens/parents/RewardsPage.tsx).

The first time the parents' corner opens on a device (not when it opens to add a child), a card explains the same in three lines: a task earns a seed, a bolt or a bell, a new level a sticker, and everything only adds up. "Részletek" opens Jutalmak; either button, or Escape, closes it for good on that device (src/screens/parents/RewardsIntro.tsx).

## Printable sheets

**Nyomtatás** (printable sheets): every game as an A4 worksheet for play without a device, one game per printout. It is in the parents' area, and also open without the gate from a Nyomtatás button on Ki játszik? and on both home screens (a full-screen Feladatlapok view with a child switch; like the parents' area it pauses the play clock). The tasks come from the games' own generators at the child's current level (a parent can pick another), and tapping becomes circling, colouring, drawing a line or crossing out: Számmagok shows the number (in the shape Mézút teaches) and the child colours that many seeds, Locsolás colours N drops in a ten-frame, Mézút traces each digit on a dotted trail with start dots and arrows and then writes it alone, Robotút draws the route and the program cards, Válogatógép joins each part to its box, Tükörgép colours the reflected lamps, Kódzár draws or colours the meaning into the box under each sign (on Hiányzó jel the covered key row first), Hibavadász crosses out the wrong card and draws the right one under it, and so on. Parents choose how many tasks go on a page (Kevesebb, nagyobb / Normál / Sok, kisebb) and up to five pages, each with new tasks. Every task has a star to colour when it is done, where colour is the task (Válogatógép, Mintagép, Tükörgép's coloured pictures, Kódzár's lamps) red is also striped and blue dotted, with a key on the sheet, so black-and-white printers work too; and every page carries the Kalandka logo, the world's name and the address the app runs at. The answers show only on screen (Feladatlap / Megoldások), never on paper; the last choice per child and game is kept on the device so a finished sheet can be checked later (src/print). Each world's shelf starts with a **Vegyes kör** sheet: every cell a task from another of that world's games, with its question above it, on one of four stages (see [levels.md](levels.md#vegyes-kör)).

**Kész lap** (marking a page done): a grown-up checks a finished sheet in Nyomtatás (picking the page opens its answers below the page buttons), or scans its QR code with the tablet's camera (Lap beolvasása). Either way the page's own answers come up, each with Jó and Hibás buttons to mark against the paper ("Mind jó" marks the rest right), above a meter of the right answers its level's sticker wants; "Lap kész" waits until every answer is marked (src/screens/parents/PageCheck.tsx). The scanner is two taps from anywhere: the gate behind every lock button offers "Kész lap beolvasása helyette", and "Ki játszik?" has a Kész lap button beside Nyomtatás; either way the gate opens straight onto the camera, not the parents' corner (Nyomtatás keeps its own Beolvasás button too). The camera fills the screen and keeps watching: a scanned sheet brings up a card over it ("Megvan: Kódzár", its level, page and task count, and for whom, or that the child already has it), with the child picked below it (with siblings, a row of children, so both children's sheets go in one sitting); a new code replaces the card. The card opens the sheet's own page with the camera off: the game and page on top with the child, the meter of the level's right answers below that, centred, then the answers as small cards (several a row on a tablet, one on a phone) to mark Jó or Hibás, and a bar at the foot with how many are checked, "A többi mind jó" and "Lap kész" ("Még 3 feladat" until every answer is marked). "Kamera" or Escape goes back without counting it. After "Lap kész" the same page says how many were right and wrong and plays the reward back, then offers Bezárás and "Következő lap beolvasása", which returns to the camera. Where the browser has its own QR reader (BarcodeDetector) it is used, else jsQR; the lamp button shows where the camera has one (src/screens/parents/ScanSheet.tsx). A page counts once, and its answers count on its game's level ladder like tasks in the game, in page order: a right answer as solved without help, a wrong one as needing help. Paper only ever moves the child up: a page on the child's level carries on their row and a full row steps up, a page above it moves them only if it holds a full row of its own (they step up from the page's level), and an easier page, a finished game, Számmagok or an old sheet that joined levels changes nothing. The receipt says when a page stepped the child up (src/state/ladder.ts `afterPage`). Its level's sticker comes as in the game, where four tasks solved on a level earn it: once four answers on that level's pages were right, in any order. Wrong answers count for nothing, and the right ones add up from page to page, so short pages add up too. A page that leaves the level short still counts as finished; Számmagok has no ladder, so there finishing is enough, as in the game. Pages marked before answers were checked keep the stickers they earned (once every page of a printout is marked done, the game's sheet opens on new tasks next time, so the next printout counts again): its tasks fill the child's reward pod like solved tasks in the games, so a 9-task page with a half-full pod pays two seeds. After a page is counted it is played back once: the button turns green with a tick, the page's tasks drop one by one into the child's pod, every full pod sends a seed (or bolt, or bell) into their total, named in that world's words, then a plain summary and any new sticker: Feladatlapok (at 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21, 34 and 55 pages in each world), and the game's own level sticker for the level the page was printed on once its level has four right answers (otherwise a line says how many there are so far); with reduced motion it shows the end straight away (src/screens/parents/PageReceipt.tsx).

**Kellékek** (cut-out pages, at the bottom of Nyomtatás): three A4 pages that are the same for every child, so they have no level, no QR code and nothing to mark, only a preview and a print button. *Csepi, Csavar és Kakukk* are fold-in-half tent cards (one per world guide) that stand beside a worksheet while the grown-up reads the task in their voice; *Jelvények* has 12 Kész!, 6 Kitartó! (stuck but finished) and 6 Bátor! (tried a new game or a harder level) badges, the three guides taking turns, to stick on a finished sheet; *Kis robot és parancskártyák* has the Robotút robot in three sizes (pick the one that fits one square of the sheet's grid, which changes with level and density) and cut-out direction and turn cards for laying out a program and stepping it on paper. The Robotút sheet links to the robot page, and the parents' "Kész lap megjelölése" card links to the badges (src/print/extras.tsx).

## Screen time

Only active play counts (app visible, parents' area closed) and the count starts again each day. Two minutes before the end a badge appears and Csepi says so; at the limit a child in a game may finish the task (up to 2 minutes), then a rest screen appears that only the parents' gate dismisses. "Pihenő most" ends play for today; evening rest blocks play from 18–20:00 until 7:00. Resetting progress does not reset today's play time.
