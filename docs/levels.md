# Levels

Every game but Számmagok shares one difficulty ladder (`src/state/ladder.ts`), and the level follows the child on its own, one step at a time:

| Game | Steps up after | Steps down after |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Locsolás, Virágsor, Villámszem | 4 answers in a row without help (7 on Virágsor's two mixed levels, so each of its seven shapes comes up once) | 3 in a row that needed help |
| Mézút | 5 digits in a row without help (a whole level's digits on the first four levels) | 3 in a row that needed help |
| Méhkas, Cserépbarátok | 5 answers in a row without help | 3 in a row that needed help |
| Robotműhely games | 4 rounds in a row without help (one slip in a round is fine) | 2 rounds in a row that needed help |
| Óratorony games | 4 answers in a row without help | 3 in a row that needed help |

"Needed help" means a wrong pick, a lit-up answer or "Mutasd!". The level meter shows the level as a staircase whose current step fills up with the streak. A step up plays once in the meter itself, about three seconds, with no card between rounds: the full step locks in dark, a star hops onto the next step, which lifts and takes the current outline, an "Új szint!" chip shows under the meter and Csepi says it. A step down is quiet ("A következőt egy kicsit könnyebben gyakoroljuk."). After any step there are three tasks before the next step down, going back up after a step down takes one more in a row, and the game never steps below the starting level a parent set in Szintek. The level is kept apart from the solved counts, so a step down never takes back a sticker, a seed or a bolt; stickers come from the highest level reached. A new child starts each ladder at a level for their age, deliberately low and with no floor, so they can still step down. A checked paper page counts on the ladder too (see [parents.md](parents.md)): its answers walk the same rule in page order, but paper only steps up, never down, and a page below the child's level changes nothing. Vegyes kör never moves the ladder: its tasks come from levels the child has already solved. Számmagok moves in order instead, through five stages of five rounds: 1–5, 6–10, 11–15, 16–20 and Vegyesen, five numbers from 1–20 in mixed order; then it starts again at 1. A parent can pick any stage as the start.

## Vegyes kör

Every island's trail starts at **Vegyes kör**, where the walk from the dock comes in, before the seven games (src/components/island.tsx `MixSpot`): a sun-gold place with the world's guide inside a loop of three colours, and no step number. It sleeps like a locked game (grey, a moon, a ring filling up) until the child has solved a task in two of that world's games; a tap has the guide say what wakes it. It waits on that world's own games only, never on another world's. Open, it plays five tasks from the games played so far, one game after another, then a closing card with a tick for each task and the way back to the island (src/state/review.ts, src/screens/ReviewDone.tsx). Each game comes once before any comes twice and never twice in a row; Számmagok and garden games still waiting on Számmagok stay out. Each task is on a level the child has already solved in that game, at or below their own. The game screens are the games' own; in the round the level meter's row fills one slot per task, the ladder never moves, and every task pays and is logged like one in the game (a finished game's practice keeps its daily cap). Back leaves the round.

On paper every world has a Vegyes kör sheet too, first on its shelf in Nyomtatás (src/print/mix.tsx): each cell is a task from a different game of the world, with that game's name and question above it, drawn and answered by the game's own sheet. Its four levels (Első lépések, Haladó, Ügyes, Mester) each take an even share of every game's sheet levels. A checked page counts and pays like any page in its world, and, like Vegyes kör in the app, moves no game's level. Its revision is worked out from its games' sheets, so it moves on whenever one of theirs does.
