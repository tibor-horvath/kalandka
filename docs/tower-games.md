# Óratorony games

Kakukk's clock tower, the third Kalandka world: times of day, the week, the seasons and the clock. Kakukk is a purple cuckoo whose crest is two clock hands. Every game has at least five levels and is open from the start: the world asks for nothing from the other worlds, so an older child can come straight here. How each game steps up and down is in [Levels](levels.md); what a solved task pays is in [Rewards](rewards.md#óratorony).

Every clock time is said through `src/lib/time.ts`, the Hungarian way: the half and the quarters name the hour the hour hand is heading to (2:30 is *fél három*, 7:15 *negyed nyolc*, 7:45 *háromnegyed nyolc*), other minutes as *három óra tíz perc*. On every dial the hour hand is short, thick and dark, and the minute hand long, thin and lighter blue, so they differ in more than colour.

| Game | Skill | How it plays |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Napirend | Order of a day, parts of the day | Pictures of a child's day (getting up, breakfast, setting off to kindergarten, lunch, playground, dinner, bath, sleep). Five levels: Mi jön utána? (one card stands in the row, pick the next of two, the other one came earlier; for 5-year-olds), three cards in order, four cards in order, Napszakok (pick reggel, délben, délután or este for a picture), Mi volt előbb? (which of two came first). A wrong card shakes once and stays dimmed; Kakukk says whether it came earlier or later; two misses light the right one up. No numbers and no clock. |
| Évszakok | Seasons, days of the week, months | Six levels: Melyik évszak? (a tree in blossom, fruit, falling leaves or snow; pick the season), Sorban jönnek (four trees in the seasons' circle, one missing), A hét napjai (the week, one day missing), Tegnap és holnap (today is marked, pick yesterday or tomorrow), Holnapután (the day after tomorrow, the day before yesterday), Hónapok (which season a month is in). Every day keeps one sign, a shape and a colour, Monday to Sunday: circle, square, triangle, star, heart, diamond, moon (`DaySign`), with its name small under it, so a child who can't read finds the day by its sign and its place. On Tegnap és holnap and Holnapután the whole week is the row of cards, "ma" over today. On Hónapok the year shows as twelve numbered tiles with the asked month lit; Kakukk says its name. |
| Óraállító | Setting a clock | A cuckoo clock: the child drags its hands (or nudges them with the arrow buttons) and presses Kész. Five levels: only the short hand on whole hours, both hands on whole hours, halves, quarters, five minutes. The long hand snaps to quarters until the last level. On a whole hour the cuckoo calls the hour ("Kakukk, kakukk, kakukk!"). A wrong clock gets one hint at a time, the minute hand first; two misses light up the number. |
| Hány óra? | Reading a clock | Kakukk says a time ("Melyik órán van fél három?") and the child picks the clock that shows it from three dials, so reading the clock never needs reading words. The same five steps as Óraállító. The wrong cards are the usual mix-ups: the hour left instead of the coming one (*fél kettő* for 2:30), the hands read the other way round, the other quarter; on Öt percek always the long hand's number taken for the minutes (*három óra négy perc* for 3:20), which Kakukk answers with "minden szám öt percet ér". After a miss on a half or quarter hour every dial tints the way between the two numbers the hour hand should stand between. |
| Digitóra | Dial and digital display | A dial, and three digital displays; Kakukk says the time (no words under the dial), and on Délután a sun sign stands under it. Whole hours, halves, quarters, five minutes, then Délután: the afternoon's 24-hour reading (*délután három óra* is 15:00). The wrong displays are the same mix-ups as in Hány óra?; on Délután one is always the morning reading (3:00 for 15:00), the twelve not added, and another an hour off. |
| Kakukk alszik | Time gone by | Two clocks: Kakukk lay down, Kakukk woke up; how long did he sleep? Five levels: one to three hours, up to five, over twelve o'clock, half hours (*másfél óra*), then Mikor ébredt? the other way round: one clock and how long he slept, and the child picks when he woke from three clocks (an hour too early and an hour too late are the wrong cards). How long is never a word: the cards are rows of hour blocks, half a block for the half hour, the number small under them. After a miss the first dial tints the hour hand's way to the second. |
| Homokóra | How long things take | No clock and no numbers. Five levels: which of two takes longer, which is shorter (the two always at least ten times apart: a clap and a bath, brushing teeth and a night's sleep, a day at kindergarten and washing hands), then the longest and the shortest of three (always a moment, a few minutes and hours), and last whether something takes a moment, minutes or hours, picked from pictures: a blink, an hourglass running out, the sun crossing to the moon (`SpanSign`). |

Every solved task fills half of the reward pod, and every second one pays a bell (see [Rewards](rewards.md#óratorony)).

## Szint-bemutató (level intros)

A level that brings a new idea (the hour hand, whole hours, fél, negyed, five minutes, the digital display, 13 óra,
counting hours slept, the day parts, the seasons, the days of the week, the months…) opens with Kakukk's slides
before its first task: 1–4 slides, turned only with Tovább and Vissza, with Kihagyom to jump straight in. Each slide
is read out once; nothing moves on its own. An idea opens by itself only once per child, in whichever game it comes
up first (`introsSeen` in the save). The yellow "?" pill in the top bar replays the level's intro, or the closest
earlier one, at any time. The content lives in `src/worlds/tower/games/intros.ts`; the slides in
`src/worlds/tower/screens/Intro.tsx`.

## Printed sheets

Each game has a sheet per level (`src/worlds/tower/print.tsx`), drawn from the same task generators so a page matches the child's level: Napirend's cards get a box to write 1, 2, 3 under them (the cards given in the app carry their number already, a card that does not belong is crossed out on the answers); Óraállító's dials have no hands and the child draws them; the other games circle the right card. The cards match the game's: Hány óra? prints the time for the grown-up to read aloud over three clocks to circle, Kakukk alszik prints hour blocks or clocks, Homokóra's last level the three pictures, and Évszakok the day signs and the year's twelve tiles. The Óratorony's Feladatlapok row in the album counts these pages.
